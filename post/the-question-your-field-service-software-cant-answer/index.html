The Question Your Field Service Software Can't Answer

A pest control operator asked a simple question — how does this month compare to last year? Their software had no answer. Not hidden. Nonexistent. Here's what we found, and the fix that now ships with every dashboard we build.

Jul 21, 2026 4 min read
📈

The Question

A pest control operator in California's Central Valley — roughly 2,500 active customers, a couple million dollars in annual recurring revenue — was looking at the operations dashboard we run for them and asked the most natural question in business:

"Can you show me how these numbers compare to last year?"

Month-over-month. Year-over-year. Plus or minus. The kind of trend line every owner assumes is sitting in their software somewhere, waiting for the right report to surface it.

It wasn't sitting anywhere. And the reason why turned into one of the most important discoveries we've made about how field service software actually works. (For the full deep dive with the numbers, see Case Study #6: The Longitudinal Record.)

What We Found

Their entire operation runs on a leading pest-industry platform — routing, scheduling, billing, the works. It is genuinely excellent at that job, and we'd never suggest replacing it. But when we went digging for historical data to build the comparison, we hit something structural:

The software stores today. It does not store history.

  • Autopay status? Overwritten in place. There is no record of what it was last month.
  • Account balances and aging? Current values only. Yesterday's balance no longer exists.
  • Customer status? More than 4,000 of their ~6,900 inactive records didn't even carry a date for when the customer left.

This isn't a bug, and it isn't unique to one vendor — it's how operational software is built across the industry. A routing platform's job is to answer "what needs to happen today?" and it answers that brilliantly. But that means the question "what was my monthly recurring revenue last June?" has no answer. Not hidden. Not hard to reach. Nonexistent. No report, no export, no API call can produce a number the system never wrote down.

Every month that passes without someone recording the trend line is a month of business history gone permanently.

The 90-Day Window

There was one partial exception: recent cancellation records still held their dates, which meant the last 90 days could be reconstructed — carefully — from raw account data. We rebuilt the operator's key metrics as they stood 30 days earlier and validated the method by reconstructing "today" the same way: it matched the live dashboard to the penny.

That gave us honest month-over-month deltas, and we shipped them to the operator's scoreboard the same day: recurring revenue up, active customer count slightly down, cancellation rate a hair above the same window last month — each number now carrying a plus-or-minus against the prior period, color-coded by whether the movement is good news or bad.

But 90 days was the hard ceiling. Beyond it, the records simply don't exist. Reconstruction could buy us a month of hindsight — it could never buy us a year.

The Fix: A System of Record for History

So we changed what our platform does with the data it already syncs. Three times a day, our integration pulls a complete snapshot of the operator's business — every subscription, every account, every metric. Now, once per day, it banks a permanent point-in-time record: recurring revenue, active customers, cancellation rate, autopay coverage, route density, completion rate — nine core numbers, written down and never overwritten.

It runs automatically. Nobody has to remember it, trigger it, or maintain it — and every new client we onboard inherits it from day one, because it's built into the sync itself rather than bolted on afterward.

The result is something the operator's own software cannot produce at any price: a longitudinal record of the business. Thirty days in, every metric gets an honest month-over-month comparison from stored truth instead of reconstruction. A year in, true year-over-year — the exact question that started all this — answers itself.

Why This Compounds

Here is the part worth sitting with: this data cannot be backfilled. If you start recording in July, your record starts in July — forever. There is no catching up, no buying the missing months, no clever engineering that recovers a number nobody wrote down.

That cuts both ways. For the operator who starts today, every day of history is an asset their competitors down the road don't have — the ability to see seasonality, to catch a slow leak in autopay coverage, to know whether this summer is actually better than last summer or just feels that way. For the operator who waits, the meter is running in reverse.

Trend intelligence isn't a report you run. It's a discipline someone has to be doing every single day — and in our experience, almost nobody is.

The Takeaway

Your field service software runs your day superbly. But ask yourself one question: could you compare the state of your business — active customers, autopay coverage, your recurring book — to the same month last year?

Your invoices remember revenue. Almost nothing remembers state — those numbers are overwritten in place daily — and every month that stays true costs you a month of trend line you will never get back. The fix isn't switching platforms. It's putting a layer alongside your existing stack that records what your operational software was never built to remember — automatically, daily, from day one.
