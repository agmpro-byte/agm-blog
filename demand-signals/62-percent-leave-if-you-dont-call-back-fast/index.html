Issue 2 · Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 4-minute read

1
The Signal

Homeowners now drop a home-service company that doesn't call back fast — and most won't even look at a business with a thin review file.

62%

A compiled 2026 stats page citing BrightLocal, Elevate Market Research and Modernize Homeowner Insights found 62% of homeowners move on if they don't hear back quickly, and 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Only 10.96% prefer online booking first.

Source: Fasite / Plumbing Perspective

2
The Owner’s Check

How long did the slowest callback of last week actually take — and who let it sit?

If you can name the job and the hours in 30 seconds, you can see your business. If you'd have to scroll back through a phone and an inbox, that's the gap.

3
What it means

The phone is still the gate. Homeowners aren't waiting for a booking form — they're calling two or three companies and keeping whoever answers. Two things decide it: how fast a human responds and whether your review file looks recent and deep enough to pass a glance. Note the scope — these are national consumer surveys, not your market. But the benchmark is the same everywhere: fewer than 20 reviews and a slow callback kill the job before price comes up. The KPIs behind callback speed →

4
Your move this week

Time your own callbacks this week — pull every inbound from the last seven days and write down the minutes to first human contact. The 2026 benchmark cited from Harvard Business Review is a live answer on calls and under 5 minutes on forms or texts during business hours. You can't fix a number you've never measured.

5
Field notes

What owners are saying in public this week: “app does not show ME the owner what invoices are sent. lets say im trying to send a full day of invoices from my phone, laptop is dead. I can't tell which ones are already sent!!” — a home-service owner reviewing Housecall Pro on Google Play, the public post. Visibility complaints — can't see what's sent, can't see a manager's schedule — run through four of this fortnight's missing-feature reviews.

6
Quick signals
•Google books jobs: On November 4, Housecall Pro and Jobber each announced integrations piping Google AI Mode booking requests into their inboxes — no lead fees. source
•Reviews go stale: BrightLocal's 2026 survey: 32% of consumers look only at reviews from the last two weeks, up from 20% a year earlier. source
•HVAC parts up again: November manufacturer increases run 3% to 15% where disclosed — audit parts costs before you quote another install. source
•Remodeling flat: NAHB's model shows Q2 2026 remodeling spending flat nationally — 0.0% average state growth, declines in 26 states. source
•Rates ease slightly: Freddie Mac's November 5 survey puts the 30-year fixed at 7.18% — still 96 basis points above a year ago. source
